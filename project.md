What the platform is — one paragraph
Apna Verdict is an anonymous life-dilemma judgment platform built for Indian Gen Z. Users submit real situations from their lives — friendship conflicts, family pressure, relationship drama. # 🎯 Gen Z UX Principles — What Makes This Feel Native to Them

## 1. No Login Wall at Entry

Users can browse the feed and vote without signing up. Account creation is only required when they want to view their Judge Score or build their identity. This ensures zero friction for the first interaction and delivers an instant dopamine hit.

**Psychology:** Foot-in-the-door — small commitment first, deeper engagement later.

---

## 2. Hinglish UI Copy Throughout

The platform uses conversational Hinglish instead of formal language.
For example:

* “Verdict do” instead of “Submit”
* “Teri galti” instead of “Your fault”
* “Bhai sahi bola” instead of “Correct”

Every word feels native and relatable, not translated.

**Psychology:** In-group belonging — the platform speaks like a friend, not a system.

---

## 3. Split Bar Shown Before Voting

Users see the current vote distribution (e.g., “58% vs 29%”) before casting their vote. This creates curiosity and subtle pressure to participate.

It interrupts passive scrolling and encourages active decision-making.

**Psychology:** Curiosity gap + completion instinct.

---

## 4. Time-Left Badges on Every Case

Each case displays a visible countdown (e.g., “3h left”). Cases automatically expire in 48 hours.

This creates urgency without being aggressive. Once a case closes, the verdict is final — and missed participation cannot be recovered.

**Psychology:** Scarcity + loss aversion.

---

## 5. Share Card = User Identity, Not Just Content

The share card highlights the user’s decision:
“Maine kaha: Uski galti — 61% India agrees.”

The focus is on the user’s opinion, not the platform. Users are not sharing content — they are expressing identity.

**Psychology:** Self-expression over content sharing.

---

## 6. Always-Visible Progress Bar

The profile continuously displays progress toward the next badge:
“353 more verdicts to unlock Street Smart.”

The goal is always specific and measurable, making progress feel achievable.

**Psychology:** Goal gradient effect — effort increases as users get closer to completion.

---

# 🧠 Core UX Insight

Every screen in the product is designed to serve a specific psychological need:

* **Feed:** Drives FOMO and curiosity
* **Verdict Screen:** Enables expression and the need to be heard
* **Results Screen:** Provides validation and a shareable outcome
* **Profile:** Builds identity and status

This is not just interface design — it is **behavior-driven UX**, where each element triggers a deliberate psychological response.

# 🇮🇳 Why This Works Specifically for Indian Gen Z

## 1. Hinglish is Not a Style — It’s Identity

The language makes the platform feel native and relatable. Without it, the product would feel distant and generic.

---

## 2. City-Based Splits Drive Engagement

Displaying opinions by city (e.g., Delhi vs Lucknow) activates regional pride and debate, which significantly increases sharing and interaction.

---

## 3. 48-Hour Expiry Mimics Live Events

The time-bound nature of cases creates urgency similar to live experiences like cricket matches — users feel compelled to participate before the moment passes.
